08 · Case study

Jukoï Capital

Quantitative data infrastructure & lakehouse architecture

Quant Researcher · Off-Cycle · Monaco · June 2025 – Present

During my off-cycle experience at Jukoï Capital in Monaco, I worked on the design and development of a provider-agnostic financial data infrastructure supporting quantitative research, market-data processing, portfolio workflows, risk calculations and pricing models.

The initial project focused on automating the ingestion, transformation and normalization of market, macroeconomic and alternative data. The resulting components were integrated into a web-based financial application, connecting research workflows with operational portfolio and risk-management tools.

I am now contributing to the design of a complete quantitative lakehouse architecture intended to make the platform more reproducible, observable and production-ready. The architecture separates raw, enriched, staging, canonical and gold data layers, while preserving source objects, provider vintages, immutable releases and replayable transformations.

The target platform combines Ceph RGW for sovereign S3-compatible object storage, Apache Iceberg for transactional analytical tables and Lakekeeper as the central Iceberg REST catalog. Trino, Spark, Polars and DuckDB provide shared SQL, distributed processing and local iteration, while Dagster orchestrates partitioned data assets and enforces data contracts.

A major focus of the architecture is financial reproducibility. Critical datasets distinguish between event time, publication time and verified availability time. Point-in-time feature construction and anti-look-ahead tests ensure that backtests only use information that would genuinely have been available at the time of each historical decision.

The platform also introduces controlled AI-assisted workflows. Local language models can support document extraction, data cleaning, normalization, field mapping and pattern detection, but their outputs remain restricted to enriched or staging layers until they pass deterministic validation, quality checks and, where required, human review.

The broader system includes lineage tracking, data-quality contracts, observability, agent evaluation, vector-search synchronization and disaster-recovery procedures. The objective is not to reproduce a monolithic data platform, but to build a modular and auditable foundation adapted to quantitative research and portfolio operations.

Challenge

Build a scalable and provider-agnostic financial data platform capable of handling market data, macroeconomic data, alternative data, portfolio processes and quantitative research while preserving reproducibility, security and temporal correctness.

Solution

Designed a layered quantitative lakehouse based on Ceph RGW, Apache Iceberg and Lakekeeper, with Dagster orchestration, Trino and Spark processing, declarative data contracts, OpenLineage tracking, Feast point-in-time features and controlled MLflow-based AI evaluation.

Impact

Established a reusable foundation for certified datasets, reproducible backtests, auditable research releases and more reliable connections between data engineering, quantitative research, trading and portfolio-management workflows.
